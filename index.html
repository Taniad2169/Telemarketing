♿ What Is ADA and WCAG?

The ADA is a U.S. civil-rights law that prohibits disability discrimination — and it applies to websites. WCAG is an international set of technical guidelines for making digital content accessible.

  • ADA — the law. It's the legal reason a website needs to be accessible.
  • WCAG — the how-to. The technical standard that shows you what “accessible” actually means.
  • WCAG has three conformance levels: Level A (minimum), Level AA (the standard most businesses are held to), and Level AAA (highest / strictest).

Simple way to say it on a call: “The ADA is the law that says your site has to be accessible. WCAG is the checklist that tells you how to get there — and most businesses are expected to meet Level AA.”

🍪 What Is a Cookie Policy for Websites?

A cookie policy is a legal document that explains how a website uses small data files called cookies to track, collect, and store visitor information.

📄 What a Cookie Policy Contains

1. Definition of Cookies

Explains what cookies are and why the site uses them.

2. Types of Cookies Used

Categorizes cookies into functional groups:

  • Strictly Necessary — required for the site to work at all.
  • Performance / Analytics — tracks how visitors use the site.
  • Functionality — remembers settings and preferences.
  • Targeting / Advertising — used to show relevant ads.

3. Lifespan

States how long each cookie stays on your device — such as temporary session cookies or long-term persistent cookies.

4. Third-Party Access

Discloses if outside vendors (like analytics or advertising networks) can set or access cookies through the site.

5. User Control

Provides instructions on how visitors can block, delete, or withdraw consent for cookies — via browser settings or a consent banner.

🔐 What Is HIPAA?

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) is a federal law protecting sensitive patient health information from unauthorized disclosure.

Protected Health Information (PHI) includes names, medical histories, birth dates, and contact details tied to health conditions.

  • Encryption — PHI must be encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Signed vendor contracts — any third party handling PHI must sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

Simple way to say it on a call: “HIPAA is the federal law that protects patient health information. If a website collects any health details, it needs encryption and signed vendor contracts to stay compliant.”

Why This Matters on a Call

  • A missing or outdated cookie policy is one of the easiest compliance gaps to spot — and one of the easiest to fix.
